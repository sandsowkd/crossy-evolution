# Long-game training experiments

The user’s primary target is a mean score above 1,000. Generation counts alone do not measure learning cost: population size, maps per genome, episode length and inherited training all matter. All reports retain actual episodes, wall time, settings, source genomes and full population archives.

## Fixed comparison rules

The original 60-second run cannot reach 1,000: decisions take 0.2 simulated seconds, so even uninterrupted forward movement earns fewer than 300 completed rows. The long-game experiments use **600 simulated seconds (36,000 physics ticks)**, with the same terrain generation, traffic density/speed, hitboxes, swept collisions and five actions. No movement, traffic or collision setting was made easier.

The common development suite contains 192 maps: the original B2-prefix validation64, A1-prefix development64, and D7-prefix development64. These maps may select models and experiment settings. Training maps are in a separate lower30-bit range. A wider development panel now contains those192 plus576 E2-prefix maps (768 total); it also selects models and is not a final test. D9-prefix final512 maps are reserved until model selection is frozen. Earlier F3/C1 tests are historical and do not select these new models. The D1 diagnosis maps were deliberately reused for a failure-state curriculum; they are training data, not a final test.

The initial sweep used equal weighting of mean and lower-half mean. A later declared development criterion added a 1,000-point success bonus. After the user clarified that **average score is the biggest goal**, new branches select the highest raw mean across the common192 maps. These are development results until a frozen model is independently tested.

## What changed

- Observation1 retains original binary cells and landing-risk inputs. Observation2 uses continuous proximity in the cell and landing channels. Observation3 keeps binary cells and replaces five global landing risks with approximate local trajectory proximity over each candidate hop. Observation4 combines distance cells with trajectory inputs. Observation5 adds a second-hop forward traffic/obstacle preview in place of clock sine/cosine, while retaining observation3’s other channels. Observation6 replaces clock channels with previous horizontal action/blockage. Observation7 retains the clock and places previous horizontal action in the global signal channel; its spatial lane warnings remain available. All new memory-channel weights start at zero, preserving the ancestor exactly on roads for version7. Every version has658 inputs and is explicitly saved with genomes/checkpoints.
- Trajectory sensors extrapolate currently visible nearby traffic through nine samples over0.2s. They do not solve a route, inspect distant RNG, filter actions or override the network. Learned output scores directly choose UP/DOWN/LEFT/RIGHT/WAIT by argmax.
- Mutation magnitude/frequency, structural mutation, crossover and parent-pool size are explicit checkpoint parameters. Seed schedules, monitor maps and lower-tail fitness weight are serialized. Native browser/CLI continuation reproduces the tuned experiment operators.
- Exact sparse observation encoding skips inputs that a frozen network does not consume. Allocation-free road collision calculations and scalar slab tests preserve the original arithmetic and scan order. Trajectory sensor pruning skips contributions proved to be zero. Differential tests compare observations, network scores, chosen actions, physics results, state and saved outcomes against frozen pre-optimization code.
